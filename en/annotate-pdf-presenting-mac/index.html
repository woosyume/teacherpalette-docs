How to Annotate a PDF While Presenting on Mac

You are presenting a PDF and want to circle a detail, underline a key phrase, or explain an idea that is not on the page. Switching apps to make a quick mark can interrupt the conversation. A presentation whiteboard lets you keep the document and your explanation together.

Keep the explanation movingWith TeacherPalette, load a PDF into Whiteboard before you present. Move through its pages, add marks as questions come up, and keep the audience focused on the point you are explaining.

A simple workflow for presenting a PDF

  1. Open your material. Load a PDF into a Whiteboard page before the presentation. You can also bring in an image when a diagram or screenshot is part of the explanation.
  2. Present the page. Move through the document as you talk, keeping the surrounding content visible so everyone has context.
  3. Mark the point that matters. Circle a detail, underline a phrase, or draw an arrow while you explain it. The mark appears over the material, so you do not have to leave the page to make your point.
  4. Give the idea more room. Move to another Whiteboard page when the explanation needs a sketch or a larger diagram, then return to the document.
  5. Keep a copy. Export the annotated Whiteboard pages as a PDF when you want to share the explanation afterward.

Keep the PDF readable while you explain

Use a highlighter for a phrase, a shape to frame a section, or an arrow to connect two ideas. If the audience needs to inspect small text, use a magnifier to show the detail without covering the rest of the page. The goal is to make just enough of a mark to guide attention.

TeacherPalette is designed around the flow of presenting: explain over the material already on your Mac, move to a Whiteboard when you need more space, and return to the document without turning a quick annotation into an app switch.
